# What an interview briefing book is

An **interview briefing book** is a single, self-contained HTML page built to prepare a candidate for **one specific interview conversation** and to be **kept open during it**. It brings together research on the company, an analysis of what the interviewer is likely screening for, and prepared language for the candidate, organized so any part can be found in a few seconds while the call is live.

The name comes from the briefing books political staff prepare before a debate or press conference: background, the main message, prepared answers to likely questions, known weak spots and how to handle them, and questions to ask back. Sales teams call parts of this a *battlecard* (what to say and avoid on a live call) and a *talk track* (the scripted spine of a call).

## One book per conversation

It's built for one interview, not one company. A recruiter screen and a hiring-manager intro at the same company get separate books, because each stage tests different things. Material carries forward from book to book; the framing doesn't.

## What it contains

| Section | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Stage map** | Where this conversation sits in the hiring process and what this stage tests. Lives in the sticky header. |
| **Battle plan** | The talk track: an "under pressure" panel, anything to settle first, the 30-second intro, the one idea to land, the candidate's proof mapped to the posting's requirements, gaps named honestly, questions to ask, traps, comp, and a close. |
| **Call drills** | Prepared answers to likely questions: behavioral stories, how-would-you-handle scenarios, and the awkward ones (layoffs, gaps, title mismatches). |
| **What they want** | What the interviewer is screening for, each item labeled **stated** (in the posting or message), **likely** (strong inference) or **guess**. |
| **Domain primer** | The problems this company's customers or users run into, from public docs, with the candidate's relevant experience mapped onto each. Named for the role: ticket map, product primer, glossary, customer problems. |
| **Company brief** | What they do, numbers (company claims marked), funding and people, values in their own words, and sources. |
| **Before the call** | A reply that must go out first, homework in order, day-of steps. The checklist saves progress in the browser. |

## How guidance is marked

| Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Say** | Use with confidence. |
| **Frame** | A weak spot, with honest language for it. |
| **Ask** | A question for the interviewer, with its source. |
| **Avoid** | A trap, and what to do instead. |

## How answers are written

Spoken answers are **beats**: Open (the answer in one line), Build (context), Prove (what the candidate did), Land (the result, tied to this company). Each beat has a bold cue of 3–8 words for glancing, with the full sentence underneath for practice. A **Cues only** toggle hides the full sentences during the call.

## Standards

- **Glanceable under stress.** Cue words, a clickable index, cues-only mode, panic lines. If something takes more than a few seconds to find mid-call, it's in the wrong place.
- **Speakable.** Under 90 seconds per answer: answer, one example, what changed, stop.
- **Specific to this interview.** Every card should only make sense for this role at this company.
- **Built only from real material.** Claims about the candidate come from their actual history. Unknowns are highlighted gaps, never filled with plausible fiction.
- **Sourced.** Facts the candidate might say out loud carry a source type, URL and date. Docs outrank blogs. Company claims are marked.
- **Self-contained and private.** One file, works offline, light and dark mode. Kept private by default.

## What it isn't

- Not a script to read aloud. The cue words prompt the candidate's own words.
- Not a résumé summary or a general research report on the company.
- Not a coaching program or mock-interview tool. Pair it with one if you like.
- Not live AI assistance during the interview. It's the candidate's own prepared notes.

The test for any section: would the candidate use it during that conversation? If not, cut it.
